Final Project

Make something people actually watch, hear, or play, using AI models and agents. Then show how you made it, what it cost, and what it means for the business of entertainment.

Due date to be announced on Canvas

The big idea

All semester you build pieces of a new creative stack: vision, image generation, AI voices, agents, reels, games, screenplays, and AI video. Now you put it together into one finished, shareable piece of work.

Pick a format: a short film, a series of short-form videos, a music video, an album or soundtrack, a 2D or 3D game, a mobile app, or a tool that helps creators make any of these. The best projects are ones a real audience would choose to watch, hear, or play, and ones a studio, label, brand, or creator could actually use.

The work

  • Video: a short film (3–10 minutes) or a series of at least five short-form videos, posted somewhere people can watch (YouTube, TikTok, Instagram, or a shared link).
  • Music: at least three finished tracks, or a soundtrack or music video, with a link people can listen to.
  • Game: a playable 2D or 3D game, online or runnable from your repo, that takes at least five minutes to play.
  • Creator tool: an app that helps people make any of the above, plus at least one finished piece made with it.

Make it shareable. A real audience reaction (views, plays, comments, a playtest) is the strongest evidence you can bring.

The report (10–15 pages)

Write it for a smart entertainment executive who has never seen your work. Use these sections, in this order. Put screenshots and stills wherever they help.

SectionWhat goes in it
1. Title + linksProject name, your name, links to the work and the GitHub repo, and one sentence that says what you made.
2. The pitchWho is the audience? Why would they watch, listen, or play? What is the hook?
3. What is new hereWhat already exists that is close? What does your work or tool do that those don't?
4. How you made itYour creative pipeline, step by step: which models and agents you used for script, images, voice, music, video, or game, and why. Draw a diagram. Walk through one real piece from concept to finished cut.
5. Cost + timeWhat did it cost to make, in tokens, credits, and dollars? How long did it take? Compare that to making it the traditional way.
6. AudienceWho saw, heard, or played it, and what happened? Views, plays, comments, playtest notes.
7. The businessWhat does this mean for studios, labels, game companies, brands, or creators? Who wins, who loses, and what about rights, likeness, and disclosure?
8. Limits + next stepsWhat did AI do badly? What did you fix by hand? What would you make next with another month?

You can write it with AI assistance. You are responsible for every word and number in it.

The slide deck

An HTML slide deck, 10 slides max, that tells the story of your project in a few minutes. Use reveal.js: one HTML file that opens in any browser, moves with the arrow keys, and goes full screen with F. Ask your vibe coder in your own words, for example:

Make me a slide deck called slides.html using reveal.js, loaded from a CDN. 10 slides max, following the outline in my report. Put my stills, screenshots, and diagram right inside the file so it still works when emailed. Big text, and the same look as my project.

The GitHub repo

  • All the code for your app, agents, or pipeline.
  • A README.md that says what it is and how to run it, step by step (or the link, if it is online).
  • A .env.example that lists the keys needed, with no real values. Never commit your real .env or any API key.
  • Make the repo public, or if it is private, add the GitHub user zlisto as a collaborator.

Testing the app

If you built an app or game, the teaching staff will try it. Either put it online (and keep it up until grades are posted), or make it run locally from a fresh clone by following your README. If it is not running after 10 minutes of following your README, it gets 0 points for "The teaching staff can run it."

Your app should look for the .env file in your repo's folder first, then one folder above it. Use the key names PORTKEY_API_KEY or OPENAI_API_KEY, spelled exactly like that.

Tips

  • Start with the audience, then the tech. "A horror short for people who love found-footage" beats "Use Veo and an agent."
  • Lock your characters and style early. Consistency is what makes AI work look finished.
  • Save as you go. Keep prompts, drafts, and screenshots for the report and the behind-the-scenes video.
  • Finish something small first. A tight one-minute piece beats an unfinished ten-minute one. Then make it bigger.
